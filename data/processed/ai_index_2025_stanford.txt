Title: AI Index 2025: State of AI in 10 Charts
Source: https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-index-2025-state-of-ai-in-10-charts
Source Type: Academic Research Report (Stanford HAI)
Category: Trend Signals / Important Releases, Evaluation / Benchmark / Observability
Date: 2025

The Stanford Human-Centered AI (HAI) Institute's AI Index provides annual data-driven analysis of AI progress, investment, and societal impact.

Key Statistics on AI Rapid Evolution:

Inference Cost Collapse:
- Cost of querying a GPT-3.5 equivalent model (64.8% MMLU accuracy):
  - November 2022: $20 per million tokens
  - October 2024: $0.07 per million tokens (Gemini-1.5-Flash-8B)
  - Reduction: more than 280-fold in approximately 18 months
- This cost collapse is unprecedented in technology history
- Enables applications that were economically infeasible 2 years ago

Model Release Pace:
- Number of significant AI model releases has accelerated dramatically
- Both open-source and proprietary models releasing at increasing frequency
- The gap between state-of-the-art and accessible models has narrowed

Benchmark Saturation:
- Many traditional benchmarks (MMLU, HumanEval) approaching saturation
- New, harder benchmarks being created to differentiate frontier models
- Benchmark performance no longer sufficient to predict real-world utility

Investment and Adoption:
- Corporate AI investment: $252 billion in 2025 (up 44% from 2024)
- Private funding for generative AI: $33.9 billion
- 65% of enterprises deployed generative AI in production (up from scattered pilots)
- Tripling of AI startup formations

Capability Milestones:
- Multimodal AI: now standard in frontier models
- Reasoning: chain-of-thought and extended thinking now common
- Code generation: AI-assisted coding adopted by majority of developers
- Long context: 1M+ token context windows available

The Knowledge Keeping-Up Problem:
- The pace of AI advancement creates a "knowledge half-life" problem
- Skills and knowledge about specific models become outdated quickly
- Researchers and practitioners report difficulty tracking all relevant developments
- The field is moving faster than academic publication cycles
- Pre-print culture (arXiv) has become essential for staying current

Implications for Researchers:
- Traditional literature review cycles (months) are too slow
- Need for automated knowledge tracking systems (like RAG)
- Community curation (Hugging Face, Papers With Code) becoming essential
- The ability to quickly assess new models is a competitive advantage

Why This Data Matters for RAG Knowledge Bases:
- Quantifies the pace of change that makes knowledge management critical
- Provides authoritative benchmarks for comparing model generations
- Stanford HAI is a credible, citable source for academic work
- Data supports the thesis that AI is evolving faster than humans can track
